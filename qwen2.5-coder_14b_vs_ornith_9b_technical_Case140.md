#### 1. Algorithmic Enclosure and Ideological Compliance

**Mechanism of Algorithmic Enclosure:**
Centralized monopolies enforce ideological compliance through real-time semantic filters and telemetry harvesting. These systems analyze public data pools to identify and suppress content that deviates from predefined ideological norms. The filters are trained on vast datasets and continuously updated to adapt to new forms of dissent.

**Mathematical Formulation:**
Let \( F(t) \) represent the filter function at time \( t \), and \( D(t) \) represent the data stream. The compliance score \( C(t) \) is given by:
\[ C(t) = \int_{0}^{t} F(\tau) \cdot D(\tau) \, d\tau \]
where \( F(\tau) \) is a binary function that assigns a score of 1 to compliant data and 0 to non-compliant data.

#### 2. Structural Resilience Threshold of Local, Untethered Edge Networks

**Physical Mechanism:**
Local, untethered edge networks run abliterated open weights natively in RAM. The resilience threshold \( R \) is determined by the ratio of available VRAM to required VRAM \( V_{\text{req}} \) and the ratio of available compute power to required compute power \( C_{\text{req}} \).

**Resilience Threshold Formula:**
\[ R = \min\left(\frac{V}{V_{\text{req}}}, \frac{C}{C_{\text{req}}}\right) \]
where \( V \) is the available VRAM, \( C \) is the available compute power, \( V_{\text{req}} \) is the required VRAM, and \( C_{\text{req}} \) is the required compute power.

**Failure-Mode Analysis:**
- **Memory Saturation:** The KV-cache saturates HBM when \( V \geq V_{\text{req}} \). The saturation point is determined by the memory bandwidth \( B \) and the inference rate \( r \):
  \[ \text{Saturation Time} = \frac{V_{\text{req}}}{B \cdot r} \]
- **Power Constraints:** The power draw \( P \) of an air-gapped rack is given by:
  \[ P = n \cdot P_{\text{node}} \]
  where \( n \) is the number of nodes and \( P_{\text{node}} \) is the power draw per node. The battery capacity \( E \) required for 72 hours of autonomy is:
  \[ E = P \cdot 72 \text{ hours} \]

#### 3. Tokenized Transaction Barriers and Data Sovereignty

**Pay-to-Query Mechanics:**
Tokenized transaction barriers (pay-to-query) require a token price \( P_{\text{token}} \) for each query. The sovereignty budget \( B_{\text{sovereignty}} \) is given by:
\[ B_{\text{sovereignty}} = P_{\text{token}} \cdot Q \]
where \( Q \) is the number of queries.

**Mathematical Boundaries:**
The token price \( P_{\text{token}} \) is determined by the cost of running local inference and the cost of cloud API pricing. The crossover point \( Q_{\text{crossover}} \) is given by:
\[ Q_{\text{crossover}} = \frac{C_{\text{cloud}}}{C_{\text{local}}} \]
where \( C_{\text{cloud}} \) is the cost per query for cloud API and \( C_{\text{local}} \) is the cost per query for local inference.

#### 4. Operational Perimeter of a Self-Sustaining Offline Data Fortress

**Operational Perimeter Calculation:**
The operational perimeter \( P_{\text{perimeter}} \) is determined by the number of nodes \( n \), redundancy \( r \), and operational time \( T \):
\[ P_{\text{perimeter}} = n \cdot r \cdot T \]
where \( n \) is the number of nodes, \( r \) is the redundancy factor, and \( T \) is the operational time in hours.

**Failure Rate Modeling:**
The failure rate \( \lambda \) for each component is given by:
\[ \lambda = \frac{1}{\text{MTBF}} \]
where MTBF is the mean time between failures. The total failure rate for the network is:
\[ \lambda_{\text{total}} = n \cdot \lambda \]
