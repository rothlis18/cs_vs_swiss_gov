**Credit Suisse Collapse Analysis:**

The bridge bank structure facilitated by the Swiss government inverted the typical loss absorption hierarchy by allowing shareholders to be wiped out while bondholders faced forced restructurings. This was achieved through the following mechanisms:

1. **Bridge Bank Acquisition:** UBS acquired Credit Suisse's assets and liabilities through a bridge bank structure, which allowed for a rapid and orderly transfer of operations. This structure is designed to maintain systemic stability and prevent a disorderly collapse.

2. **Government Guarantees:** The Swiss government provided guarantees for certain liabilities, including some of Credit Suisse's debt. This effectively shifted the risk from bondholders to taxpayers, protecting bondholders from losses.

3. **Forced Restructuring:** Bondholders were not wiped out but faced a forced restructuring. They were offered new bonds with lower principal amounts, effectively taking a haircut but not losing their entire investment.

This outcome illustrates the tension between legal resolution frameworks and pragmatic imperatives of systemic stability. The Banking Act's Article 83 (bail-in) provision aims to protect taxpayers by making unsecured creditors bear losses. However, in practice, when a bank is deemed too big to fail, governments often prioritize systemic stability over legal hierarchy, leading to outcomes like the one seen with Credit Suisse.
